## Abilities

**1.1** If information in this Rules Reference contradicts the Learn to Play booklet, the Rules Reference takes precedence.

**1.2** If a card ability contradicts information in the Rules Reference, the card takes precedence. If both the card and the rules can be followed at the same time, they should be.

**1.3** Each ability describes when and how a player can resolve it.
- If an ability with a specified duration is resolved, the effect of the ability remains through that duration, even if the component that caused the ability is removed.

**1.4** If a card has multiple abilities, each ability is presented as its own paragraph.

**1.5** If an ability contains the word "Action," a player must use a component action during the action phase to resolve that ability.

**1.6** If an ability uses the word "cannot," that effect is absolute.

**1.7** If an ability uses the word "may," that effect is optional.

**1.8** If an ability uses the word "must," that effect is mandatory.

**1.9** If an ability uses the word "cannot," that effect is absolute.

## Action Phase

**3.1** During a player's turn, they may perform one of the following three types of actions:
- A strategic action
- A tactical action  
- A component action

**3.2** If a player cannot perform an action, they must pass.

**3.3** After a player has passed, they have no further turns and cannot perform additional actions during that action phase.
- During a turn that a player passes, they can resolve transactions and "at the start of your turn" abilities.
- A player that has passed can still resolve the secondary ability of other players' strategy cards.
- It is possible for a player to perform multiple, consecutive actions during an action phase if all other players have passed during that action phase.

**3.4** A player cannot pass until they have performed the strategic action of their strategy card.
- During a three-player or four-player game, a player cannot pass until they have exhausted both of their strategy cards.

**3.5** After all players have passed, play proceeds to the status phase.

---

## Active Player

**4.1** During the action phase, the player who is first in initiative order is the first active player.

**4.2** After the active player takes a turn, the next player in initiative order becomes the active player.

**4.3** After the last player in initiative order takes a turn, the player who is first in initiative order becomes the active player again, and turns begin again in initiative order, ignoring any players who have already passed.

---

## Active System

**5.1** When a player performs a tactical action, they activate a system by placing a command token from their tactic pool in that system. That system is the active system.

**5.2** A player cannot activate a system that already contains one of their command tokens.

**5.3** A player can activate a system that contains command tokens that match other players' factions.

**5.4** A system remains the active system for the duration of the tactical action during which it was activated.

---

## Adjacency

**6.1** Two system tiles are adjacent to each other if any of the tiles' edges are touching each another.

**6.2** A system that has a wormhole is treated as being adjacent to a system that has a matching wormhole.

**6.3** A unit or planet is adjacent to all system tiles that are adjacent to the system tile that contains that unit or planet.
- A system is not adjacent to itself.

**6.4** A planet counts as being adjacent to the system that contains that planet.

**6.5** Systems that are connected by lines drawn across one or more hyperlane tiles are adjacent for all purposes.

## Agenda Cards

**7.1** There are two types of agenda cards: laws and directives.

**7.2** Laws can permanently change the rules of the game.

**7.3** When resolving a law, if a "For" outcome received the most votes, or if the law requires an election, the law's ability becomes a permanent part of the game. Players resolve the outcome and place the agenda card either in the common play area or in a player's play area, as dictated by the card.

**7.4** If a law is in a player's play area as opposed to the common play area, that player owns that law.

**7.5** If a law is discarded from play, that law's ability is no longer in effect. Place that card on the top of the agenda card discard pile.

**7.6** If an "Against" outcome of a law received the most votes, players resolve the outcome and discard the agenda.

**7.7** Directives provide one-time game effects.

**7.8** When resolving a directive, players resolve the outcome that received the most votes and discard the agenda card.

---

## Agenda Phase

**8.1** Players skip the agenda phase during the early portion of each game. After the custodians token is removed from Mecatol Rex, the agenda phase is added to each game round.

**8.2** During the agenda phase, players can cast votes on agendas that can change the rules of the game.

**8.3** Each player's influence value determines the number of votes they can cast.

**8.4** Players cast votes in initiative order, starting with the speaker.

**8.5** To cast votes, a player exhausts planets they control to gain votes equal to the planet's influence value.

**8.6** A player cannot cast votes for multiple outcomes of the same agenda. Each vote a player casts must be for the same outcome.

**8.7** Some agendas have "For" and "Against" outcomes. When a player casts votes on such an agenda, that player must cast their votes either "For" or "Against."

**8.8** Some agendas instruct players to elect either a player or a planet. When a player casts votes for such an agenda, that player must cast their vote for an eligible player or planet as described on the agenda.

**8.9** When electing a player, a player can cast votes for themselves.
- When resolving these agendas, the "elected player" is the player for whom the most votes are cast.

**8.10** When electing a planet, a player must cast votes for a planet that is controlled by a player.
- When resolving these agendas, the "elected planet" is the planet that had the most votes cast for it.

**8.11** When casting votes, a player must declare aloud the outcome for which their votes are being cast.

**8.12** Trade goods cannot be spent to cast votes.

**8.13** A player may choose to abstain by not casting any votes.

**8.14** Some game effects allow a player to cast additional votes for an outcome. These votes cannot be cast for a different outcome than other votes cast by that player.

**8.15** If a player cannot vote on an agenda because of a game effect, that player cannot cast votes for that agenda by exhausting planets or through any other game effect.

## Asteroid Fields

**11.1** A ship cannot move through or into an asteroid field.

---

## Attach

**12.1** To attach a card to a planet card, a player places the card with the attach effect partially underneath the planet card.

**12.2** If a player gains or loses control of a planet that contains a card with an attach effect, the attached card stays with that planet.
- The attached card maintains its exhausted or ready state.

## Combat

### Space Combat

**67.1** Space combat occurs when ships from different factions occupy the same system.

**67.2** During space combat, players resolve combat rounds until only one player (or no players) has ships remaining in the active system.

**67.3** Each combat round consists of the following steps:
1. **Anti-Fighter Barrage**: Players resolve anti-fighter barrage abilities.
2. **Announce Retreats**: Players announce retreats.
3. **Roll Dice**: Players roll dice for their ships.
4. **Assign Hits**: Players assign hits to opposing ships.
5. **Deploy Fighters**: Players deploy fighters.
6. **End of Combat Round**: Players check if combat continues.

### Ground Combat

**68.1** Ground combat occurs when ground forces from different factions occupy the same planet.

**68.2** During ground combat, players resolve combat rounds until only one player (or no players) has ground forces remaining on the planet.

**68.3** Each combat round consists of the following steps:
1. **Announce Retreats**: Players announce retreats.
2. **Roll Dice**: Players roll dice for their ground forces.
3. **Assign Hits**: Players assign hits to opposing ground forces.
4. **End of Combat Round**: Players check if combat continues.

## Command Tokens

**13.1** Command tokens are used to activate systems and track strategy card usage.

**13.2** Each player has three command pools:
- **Strategy Pool**: Used for strategy cards
- **Tactics Pool**: Used for tactical actions
- **Fleet Pool**: Used for fleet supply

**13.3** Command tokens are placed on the game board to activate systems and are returned to players during the status phase.

---

## Components

**14.1** The game includes various components:
- **Faction Sheets**: Each player's unique abilities and starting units
- **Strategy Cards**: Provide special actions and determine turn order
- **Action Cards**: Provide one-time abilities
- **Agenda Cards**: Laws and directives voted on during the agenda phase
- **Promissory Notes**: Tradeable abilities between players
- **Technology Cards**: Provide permanent upgrades
- **Unit Upgrade Cards**: Improve existing units
- **System Tiles**: The game board
- **Planet Cards**: Individual planets with resources and influence
- **Command Tokens**: Track actions and fleet supply
- **Control Tokens**: Mark controlled planets
- **Trade Goods**: Currency for various actions
- **Commodities**: Tradeable resources
- **Dice**: Used for combat and other random effects

---

## Costs

**15.1** Some abilities have costs that must be paid to resolve their effects.

**15.2** Common costs include:
- Spending resources or trade goods
- Spending command tokens
- Exhausting or purging cards
- Activating specific systems

**15.3** If a player cannot pay the cost of an ability, they cannot resolve that ability.

## Game Phases

### Strategy Phase

**16.1** During the strategy phase, players choose strategy cards in initiative order.

**16.2** Each strategy card provides a primary ability and a secondary ability.

**16.3** Players resolve the primary ability of their chosen strategy card during the action phase.

**16.4** Other players can resolve the secondary ability of strategy cards during the action phase.

### Action Phase

**16.5** During the action phase, players take turns performing actions.

**16.6** Players can perform strategic actions, tactical actions, or component actions.

**16.7** Players continue taking turns until all players have passed.

### Status Phase

**16.8** During the status phase, players:
- Score objectives
- Draw action cards
- Redistribute command tokens
- Prepare for the next round

### Agenda Phase

**16.9** The agenda phase occurs after a player controls Mecatol Rex.

**16.10** During the agenda phase, players vote on agenda cards.

---

## Objectives

**17.1** There are two types of objectives: public objectives and secret objectives.

**17.2** Public objectives are revealed progressively throughout the game.

**17.3** Secret objectives are assigned individually to players.

**17.4** Players score victory points by meeting objective conditions.

**17.5** Some objectives are scored immediately, while others are scored during the status phase.

---

## Technology

**18.1** Technology provides permanent upgrades to players' capabilities.

**18.2** Technologies have prerequisites that must be met before they can be researched.

**18.3** Technologies are categorized into different types:
- **Unit Upgrades**: Improve existing units
- **Faction Technologies**: Unique to specific factions
- **Generic Technologies**: Available to all factions

**18.4** Players research technologies by spending resources and meeting prerequisites.

---

## Trade

**19.1** Players can trade commodities and trade goods with each other.

**19.2** Commodities can be converted into trade goods.

**19.3** Trade goods can be spent on various game actions.

**19.4** Players can negotiate trades during the action phase.

**19.5** Some abilities allow players to gain additional trade goods or commodities.

---

## Victory

**20.1** The game ends when a player reaches the required number of victory points.

**20.2** The standard victory condition is 10 victory points.

**20.3** Victory points are gained primarily through scoring objectives.

**20.4** Some abilities and agenda cards can provide additional victory points.

**20.5** The player with the most victory points at the end of the game wins.

---

## Thunder's Edge & Expeditions

**21.1** At the end of your turn, you may perform **one expedition** by claiming an unclaimed slice on the expedition token and paying its cost (5 resources; discard 2 action cards; 5 influence; discard 1 unscored secret objective; exhaust a tech-specialty planet; or spend 3 trade goods).

**21.2** Claiming a slice gives you your faction **breakthrough** (if you don't have it) and may trigger a breakthrough roll for **The Fracture** (on a 1 or 10).

**21.3** A slice can only be claimed once; a player can claim multiple slices over the game (max one per turn).

**21.4** When the **sixth slice** is claimed, flip the token to **Thunder's Edge** and place it in an empty system without a planet, supernova, or printed wormhole (and not in The Fracture). The player with the **most claimed slices** places infantry on Thunder's Edge equal to their claimed slices and gains control of the planet.

**21.5** If Thunder's Edge is placed on a frontier token, the token stays and can still be explored (e.g., with *Dark Energy Tap*).

**21.6** After Thunder's Edge is on the board, expeditions no longer grant breakthroughs to players who haven't gained theirs yet.

**21.7** If The Fracture enters play, place ingress tokens per the triggering effect; each ingress is adjacent to every egress printed in The Fracture. If Thunder's Edge is in play when The Fracture appears, place an ingress token in the Thunder's Edge system (otherwise in Mecatol Rex).

## Additional Rules

### Timing

**22.1** Effects that occur "when" an event happens take priority over those that occur "after" the event.

**22.2** During the action phase, players resolve abilities in initiative order, starting with the active player.

**22.3** During the strategy or agenda phases, players resolve abilities starting with the speaker and proceeding clockwise.

### Multiple Abilities

**22.4** If multiple abilities would resolve at the same time, players resolve them in initiative order.

**22.5** Players must resolve abilities in their entirety unless parts are optional (preceded by "may").

### Component-Specific Rules

**22.6** **Action Cards**: The opening paragraph describes when the ability can be resolved.

**22.7** **Promissory Notes**: Most have abilities that specify when they can be resolved; some trigger upon receipt.

**22.8** **Agenda Cards**: Abilities correspond to outcomes and are resolved during the agenda phase after voting.

**22.9** **Faction Abilities**: Presented on faction sheets, including unique flagship abilities and perpetual effects.

**22.10** **Unit Abilities**: Named abilities presented above a unit's attributes on faction sheets or unit upgrade cards.
